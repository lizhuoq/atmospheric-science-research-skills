# Atmospheric Peer Review

Evaluate whether the central claims follow from the study design, data, analysis, validation, and uncertainty. Review the work presented, not the authors.

## Workflow

1. Identify manuscript type, scope, central claims, contribution, evidence chain, and what decisions the review must support. Do not begin with line editing.
2. Build a fact base from the manuscript: data, domain/period, variables, instruments/models, experiments, statistics, validation, results, figures/tables, uncertainty, and stated limitations.
3. Test internal consistency across abstract, methods, results, figures, supplements, data/code statements, and conclusions.
4. Apply domain gates as relevant: scale and representativeness, observation–model consistency, forcing/boundary assumptions, parameterization, calibration/validation independence, retrieval chain, dependence, leakage, multiple testing, attribution, and causal language.
5. Examine whether controls and baselines distinguish the proposed mechanism from alternatives. Check robustness, negative results, sensitivity, sample attrition, and uncertainty propagation.
6. Verify a sample of consequential citations and all challenged source claims when access permits. Mark unverifiable points rather than guessing.
7. Classify concerns by consequence: `critical` invalidates the central inference; `major` requires substantial analysis or qualification; `minor` improves clarity/reproducibility without changing the main inference.
8. For each concern, state the affected claim, evidence pointer, technical reason, consequence, and a feasible revision or analysis path. Avoid duplicate or quota-driven concerns.
9. End with strengths, limitations of the review, and a conditional recommendation if requested. An editorial decision is not a scientific certainty.

## Output

- Manuscript and central-claim summary
- Major strengths
- Prioritized concerns with evidence pointers and revision paths
- Minor and presentation comments
- Citation/reproducibility/integrity checks
- Review limitations and conditional recommendation

## Quality gates

Never invent missing manuscript content, sources, analyses, or ethical violations. Do not demand preferred methods when the chosen method is valid. Do not hide a validity problem inside stylistic feedback.

## Example

`Use atmospheric-peer-review for a pre-submission review of this regional climate attribution manuscript, emphasizing internal variability, model adequacy, and causal overreach.`
